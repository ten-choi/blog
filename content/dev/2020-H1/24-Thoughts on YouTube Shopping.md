YouTube Shopping already exists in the US and is being tested in Korea.

Any IT company with foresight is probably already working on related projects. Naver and Kakao, being domestic companies, can move

quickly on plans built around the Korean market. YouTube is different. It has more resources and big data than any Korean company,

but as a global company, it's bound to be slower at rolling out a business in Korea. So what should we be thinking about?

There probably aren't many third parties yet figuring out how to make money from YouTube Shopping. But as a programmer,

it's not obvious what I could do either.

Things anyone could do (the key is to publish the moment the system launches in Korea):

1\. Make videos explaining YouTube Shopping and how to set it up. Study the US system in advance, show how it differs,

   and explain how to list products in Korea.

2\. Make a physical, buzzworthy item that would sell on YouTube (like one of those hats where you pull a string and the bunny ears pop up).

   Make a fun, goofy, trendy item that looks cute or silly when someone uses it. (It'll take some pre-launch marketing.)

3\. Run an agency business. Just look at how agencies work on Naver Shopping and do the exact same thing on YouTube. Search Google or YouTube

   and you'll find tons of them.

Just being good at your own job isn't enough anymore.

Knowing where things are heading matters. I need to keep learning, little by little.
