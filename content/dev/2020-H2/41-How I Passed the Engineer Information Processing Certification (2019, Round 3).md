## **\- The 2019 Engineer Information Processing Exam**

I took this exam because I needed a skilled-worker visa.

You can get that visa with an IT degree alone, but I heard the Engineer Information Processing certification (Korea) would help, so I decided to go for it.

The exam has a written part and a practical part, covering a bit of coding, algorithms, databases, new technologies, and a few English questions.

People joke that you can pass it with one day of cramming, but I'm a worrier, so I studied quite a lot.

As a CS major, failing it would've been a bit embarrassing.

For the written exam, I split my time half on algorithms and half on everything else.

I spent about two weeks on that, then used the remaining two weeks to memorize every past question from 2014 Round 1 through 2019 Round 2.

Once I actually sat the exam, it was easier than I expected. Round 3 felt almost too easy...

I also studied for a month for the practical exam.

For three weeks I memorized about 90% of the terms in the Sinagong prep book (ERP, SEM, digital footprint, data mining, denormalization, DB, and so on)

in a term -> explanation format. I solved every past exam from 2015 Round 1 through 2019 Round 2.

I memorized all the answers too. I was basically at the point where you press a button and the answer pops out.

At the test center, they tell you to check your test booklet. While skimming it, I already felt like I'd passed.

Round 3 was almost anticlimactic. Honestly, I think a CS major could have passed it with just a week of study.

I finished in about 30 minutes. After my first pass I was sure I'd scored at least 80, and after that

I just double-checked about five times that I'd filled in the answer sheet correctly. (I tend to use all the time I'm given.)

I'd also signed up for the Industrial Engineer Information Processing practical exam as insurance, and that one was actually harder.

The question format was changing in 2020, so I really didn't want to fail. I wanted to get it done in one shot.

Luckily, it all worked out.

Since the format changes from 2020, the certification might become more valuable.
