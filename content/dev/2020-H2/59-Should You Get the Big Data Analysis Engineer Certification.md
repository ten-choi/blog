### **Big Data Analysis Engineer (Korean National Certification)**

####                          **To cut to the chase: I don't think now is the time.**

**\-Big Data Analysis Engineer**

 **Big data**: Data too large for traditional databases to handle comfortably, or a large collection of data that fits a purpose

               even if it isn't in a typical database format, is called **big data**.

               People who analyze how to use it efficiently and propose solutions are what I'd call

                big data analysts, and holders of this certification will probably be seen as **government-certified big data analysts**.

Personally, I think certifications are generally **a business the government runs**.

Sure, government certification is reassuring, but there are plenty of people who can do the job without one.

I've seen many of them prove it themselves.

\- That said, there are cases where, say, constructing a building requires someone with an **Electrical Engineer certification**,

or when the government puts out an **IT project**, some projects can only be won if a certain number of people on the team hold the **Engineer Information Processing certification**.

In other words, having certified people at a company can give it a slight edge when doing government-related work.

As an IT major, my mindset is: as long as you can code well, **"why would you need the Engineer Information Processing certification?"** 

Even now, I've rarely felt that certifications matter in actual work. 

Still, I do hold the **Engineer Information Processing certification**. Quite a few people I know in IT told me it's good to have. 

And looking at job postings, I can see that **Engineer Information Processing** has quietly become a **preferred or even required qualification** at some companies.

I think it's a fairly general-purpose certification that almost everyone takes a shot at.

And if you work in IT, it has clear benefits. But a big data certification?

Rather than investing time and effort in a certification that just came out, I think you're better off focusing on your current work or studying English. 

In about two years, once the patterns for the written and practical exams settle down, I'd prepare for it if I needed it. 

Pros who analyze big data already exist; the government has just turned it into a certification. 

Since there are plenty of experienced people out there, it's probably best to keep an eye on it from a distance for now and check periodically whether the certification is worth it.
