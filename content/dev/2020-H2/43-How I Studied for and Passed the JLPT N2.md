I started studying Japanese vocabulary in July 2019.

I could hold a basic conversation, but I didn't know a single katakana, hiragana, or kanji character.

My method was simple. Outside the time I spent on IT and the Engineer Information Processing certification, I studied with a Japanese vocab app.

I'd wake up, open the app, and memorize words on my way to the academy. That gave me about an hour before class.

From 9 a.m., I studied Japanese reading for two and a half hours.

With a few breaks, that took me to around noon. Even at lunch with friends from the academy, I kept the app open.

July was when I memorized the N5 vocabulary. (Until 6 p.m. I worked on other things.) After 6, I had dinner. If you bring food from home,

you can memorize words while you eat. When I'd been grinding vocab for so long that I got restless and couldn't take it anymore, I'd switch to Japanese radio

or the news. Add Japanese input to Windows and search on YouTube, and you'll find tons of fun stuff.

I went home at 11, relaxed for an hour, and went to bed. On weekends I started at noon and went home around 10.

To keep a routine like this, you can't hang out with friends.

After three months of this, N5 through N3 are manageable. I spent the remaining three months on reading and vocab; N2 has quite a few more words.

I glanced at N1 now and then, but since I was taking N2, I focused on that. N1 is honestly brutal.

I went with a ratio of roughly vocab 2 : reading 1 : grammar 0.5, then for the last two months I focused on reading and listening.

I kept the same routine. (By then the Engineer Information Processing exam was over.)

With a month and a half left, grab past N2 exams and work through them all. I think I did three years' worth, two per year, so six in total.

If there's something you don't know, memorize it or spend time on it until you understand it. Those are exactly the things that show up on the test.

Reading was my weak spot, so I spent the last month mostly on reading.

Then exam day came. I think the second 2019 session was on the easier side. Scoring myself afterward, I felt relieved.

When the results came in January, I'd gotten half the reading wrong and missed only one question elsewhere. I seriously did nothing but eat and study, so hitting my goal

felt great. Just a heads-up: the stress can make you pretty on edge.

The study method I recommend most is to start with conversation.

To me, a foreign language is for talking to people, not for certificates or writing. Even if you can't read or write it,

being able to speak is enough. That's how I studied. Of course, you'll need to study grammar eventually, but I recommend studying in a way that lets you

say what you actually want to say.
