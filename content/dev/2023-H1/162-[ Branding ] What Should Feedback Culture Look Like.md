What's the most effective way to give feedback?  
It comes from a Western culture, but Netflix has a rule called the 4As. I thought it was worth referencing, so here it is.

1.  AIM TO ASSIST: give your opinion with the intent to help (in good faith)
2.  ACTIONABLE: make it clear how the feedback helps the other person
3.  APPRECIATE: if you get good feedback, thank the person who gave it
4.  ACCEPT OR DISCARD: think the feedback through yourself, then discuss it

A culture like this is hard to establish unless team leads manage it or it's made company policy.  
That said, I'm sure it would work great if it's actually done right.

### Pros and Cons of Feedback

The downside: Netflix has a culture of letting people go if they don't meet the bar, to keep talent density high.  
For mutual feedback to become a virtuous cycle, people need to be at a similar level, and the easy firing typical of the West supports that.  
So they probably have plenty of people who can make a good culture stick.  
It's best to borrow from it in moderation. New corporate cultures pop up all the time wherever talent gathers.

Discussion aimed at finding something better is discussion, not feedback,  
so I think feedback is needed in two main cases:

1.  When you need to tell someone something they don't know
2.  When you ask someone for advice on whether there's a way to do better than you do now

Case 1 can only flow top-down. Typically it's a senior teaching a junior something.  
Case 2 is when you don't know something but someone else knows a better way.  
It's good to have a culture where anyone can do this, regardless of rank.  
And if you go to give feedback and realize your own approach isn't great, it just becomes feedback in reverse.

### Making It Work

Handing someone a legendary double-edged sword doesn't mean they'll cut down every enemy.  
You have to figure out how to fit it into your own company. If you're not a team lead, you need a strategy to bring this culture in.

1\. Pitch it to your team lead  
2\. Pitch it in meetings  
3\. Pitch it to individuals, little by little

Do a bit of all three and it might take hold.  
Places where talent gathers often already have a similar culture.  
If the soil just can't be farmed, your best bet is to leave it out of the equation.
