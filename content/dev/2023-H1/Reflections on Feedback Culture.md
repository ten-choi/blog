#### 🔖 Overview

It's important to keep thinking about what healthy feedback looks like.

#### 📒 Contents

-   Feedback culture
-   Putting it into practice

##   
\- Feedback Culture

It comes from a Western culture, but Netflix has a rule called the 4As. I thought it was worth referencing, so here it is.

-   AIM TO ASSIST: give it with the intent to help (in good faith)
-   ACTIONABLE: tell the other person what they gain from the feedback
-   APPRECIATE: thank the person who gave you feedback
-   ACCEPT OR DISCARD: when you get feedback, think about whether it's right, then discuss it

I think this culture is hard to establish unless a team lead drives it or it's made company policy.  
I'm also sure it would work great if it's actually put in place. 

The downside: Netflix has a culture of letting people go if they don't meet its bar, to keep talent density high.  
In other words, mutual feedback only becomes a virtuous cycle when people are at a similar level, so it's best to borrow from it in moderation.  
  
Corporate cultures like this pop up all the time wherever talent gathers.  
  

I think feedback is needed in two main cases (growth aimed at finding something better counts as discussion, not feedback):

1.  When there's something I don't know
2.  When there's something better than what we have now

Case 1 can only flow top-down. Typically it's a senior teaching a junior something.  
Case 2 is when I know something the other person doesn't. It's good to have a culture where anyone can do this, regardless of rank.  
And if you go to give feedback and realize your own approach isn't great, it just becomes reverse feedback.

## \- Putting It into Practice

Handing someone a legendary double-edged sword doesn't mean they'll cut down every enemy.  
You have to figure out how to fit it into your own company. If you're not a team lead, you need a strategy to bring this culture in.

1\. Pitch it to your team lead  
2\. Pitch it in meetings  
3\. Pitch it to individuals, little by little

Do a bit of all three and it might take hold.  
Places where talent gathers often already have a similar culture.  
If the soil just can't be farmed, your best bet is to leave it out of the equation.
