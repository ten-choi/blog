I joined the Halloween Open House at the Fukuoka Toastmasters Club.

It was a regular meeting held on Tuesday, October 20, from 6:30 to 8:30 p.m.
I wanted to try a different kind of event where people talk in English.
The venue was the 8th-floor practice room at AIREF, 2-5-1 Maizuru, Chuo-ku, Fukuoka. https://maps.app.goo.gl/kqFq2QSWQvbYPPwJA
It was also held online, but I went in person.
The venue is near Akasaka Station, so it was close to home. I was glad I didn't have to go far.


Japan turned out to be more passionate about English than I expected. Fukuoka isn't the capital, yet people of all ages were holding on to English. From people who looked like students to office workers and older generations, everyone sat together listening to speeches. It wasn't a room full of people who were already good at English. Still, the way each speaker pushed to finish their talk at the mic left an impression on me.

The meeting started with guest introductions. First-timers gave a short greeting, and then we moved on to the main program. True to the Halloween Open House theme, some Halloween topics were mixed in, and otherwise people gave speeches they had prepared in advance. As a guest, I listened to the speeches and took part in some of the impromptu speeches with given topics.

The prepared speeches were about picking a theme and driving your own story home within the time limit. The topic-based ones were shorter and more improvised. It wasn't a debate where people rebut each other and go back and forth. It was closer to one person speaking, everyone else listening, and the next person picking up from there.

It ran for about two hours, and the atmosphere was easygoing. At first I had expected something closer to a discussion. I thought it would be a place to use English while trading opinions, but in reality it was mostly speeches. It wasn't what I expected, but it wasn't bad. As a first-time guest, just listening and only jumping in on short topics was less pressure.

For an event I went to in order to practice English, I didn't actually say much. Even so, just listening to other people's speeches from start to finish was a good study session. I'm happy I took this chance to give it a try. If you want discussion, you can look for a meeting with a different format; if you want to experience giving speeches, this one is more than enough. At least I now know of one place near home where I can use English.

They said there was a casual after-party once the meeting ended. I only joined the meeting and skipped the party.

At the end we took a group photo. I don't want my face on my blog, so I decided not to post it.
