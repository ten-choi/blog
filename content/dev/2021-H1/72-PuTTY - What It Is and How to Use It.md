#### **\- PuTTY**

A **free application** that, instead of a physical terminal, uses a virtual terminal on an OS like Windows

to provide remote access to a server over SSH, Telnet, or raw TCP.

Terminal -> an electronic device used to enter and display data on a system such as a computer.

#### **\- How to Download It**

Search for **putty** on Google. (Google knows everything.)

![](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/ten-choi/blog-images@main/dev/tistory/72/img_1.webp)

When you open the site, you'll see the screen below. Click the arrow.

![](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/ten-choi/blog-images@main/dev/tistory/72/img_2.webp)

Do you see the screen below? I marked the spot with a red line. (Scroll down one more and there's a zoomed-in image.)

![](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/ten-choi/blog-images@main/dev/tistory/72/img_3.webp)

If you're on a typical Windows machine, click **64-bit putty.exe** to download it.

![](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/ten-choi/blog-images@main/dev/tistory/72/img_4.webp)

#### **\- How to Use It**

![](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/ten-choi/blog-images@main/dev/tistory/72/img_5.webp)

1\. These are **PuTTY**'s detailed communication settings. (I haven't needed to touch these yet.)

2\. Enter the IP and port you want to connect to. (The **port** must be open in the target server's **firewall**.)

3\. Click Open. (You'll usually connect over **SSH**.)

#### **\- Result**

Enter your login ID and password, and you're connected.

![](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/ten-choi/blog-images@main/dev/tistory/72/img_6.webp)
