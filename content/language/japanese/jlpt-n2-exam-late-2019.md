## 🎒 What to Bring

Make sure you've got these with you on exam day:

- **Your test voucher** — no voucher, no exam. Double-check for it the night before.
- **Pencils and an eraser**
- **A watch** — you can't check your phone during the test.

## 📚 Four Months of Prep

I took the N2 exam back in 2019. At the time, I was planning to move to Japan and work as a software engineer, so I had *a lot* riding on it.

Honestly, I felt like I hadn't studied enough. Strangely, though, I still felt confident — and looking back, I think I know why. I only had about **four months** to prepare, but I made every one of them count. I studied hard and stayed focused the whole way through. That's where the confidence came from.

## 🚇 Getting to the Test Venue

I took the exam in **Daejeon, Korea**, since that's where I was living. Getting around Daejeon is refreshingly simple — the subway runs on a single line, traffic is light, and you can pretty much just show up without overthinking it.

Which is probably why I cut it *way* too close and walked in **five minutes** before the exam started. Don't be like me.

> 💡 **Tip:** Aim to get there about **20 minutes early**. You'll want time to find your room, use the restroom, and settle your nerves before things kick off.

The proctors walk you through everything, so there's no need to stress about the logistics.

## ✍️ The Exam Itself

On my test date, the **listening and grammar** sections felt pretty manageable. I'd also watched a ton of Japanese videos over the years, so listening was the last thing on my mind.

**Reading** was a different story. It was packed with tough vocabulary, and when you don't know a word, there's nothing you can do about it in the moment. It's not like math, where you can reason your way to an answer — it comes down entirely to what you've already memorized. That one section aside, though, the exam wasn't as brutal as I'd expected.

## 🎉 The Result

About two months later, the results came in: **I passed!**

I'd only gotten through about half of the reading section, and I missed one question each in grammar and listening. The JLPT scores each section separately, so as long as you clear the minimum in every one and hit the overall total, you're through — which is exactly how I squeaked by. All things considered, I'm pretty happy with that.

If there's one thing this taught me, it's that **consistency is everything** when you're learning a language.

Good luck to everyone out there studying — *you've got this!* 💪
