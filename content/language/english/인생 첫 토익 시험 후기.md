For someone who barely studied English until college, that's pretty solid progress.

Floors 1 through 3 were nearly packed. If there's one pro tip, it's this: **go to the restroom on a floor with fewer people**. It felt like only first-timers at Kyushu University were lining up for the restroom. I got lucky — my test room was on the 3rd floor, and on my way up I noticed that only the 2nd floor had a line.

## The Illusion of Having Time to Spare

The exam ran from 10:20 to 12:20, and the listening section ended around 11:00 — no, more like 11:10, I think.
I thought, "Wait, I have way more time left than I expected? This is easy mode!"

I was wrong.

Part 7 was harder than I expected.
Having basic conversations with English-speaking friends doesn't mean you'll do well on the TOEIC. The kind of English is just different. How do I put it — it feels like a test of grammar and vocabulary size.

Still, working through it does help to some extent. You end up learning quite a bit thanks to all the unfamiliar words.

In the end, I straight-up guessed on 30 questions. If I had been serious about preparing, I would have done practice tests on my own weeks in advance with something like the 2024 TOEIC samples — but this time, I basically paid money to buy experience points. I'm going to take it again later anyway.

It felt like a dry run for the "real" attempt next time, when I'll be better prepared — getting my body used to the route to the test site, where to buy water, where to catch the bus, that kind of thing.

## Notes for the Next Exam

Thinking I'd have time to spare, my mind kept wandering — but in reality, there's no room for that. You have to solve questions like a machine. When it's over, you barely remember anything.

- Time management: For example, if you're on the 170s with only 5 minutes left, bubble in answers for everything from 175 onward first, then go back and actually solve the 170s.
- Priorities: I've heard that on the TOEIC, you can't afford to miss the questions everyone else gets right. In other words, not missing the early- and mid-section questions matters more.
- The bottom line: long-passage reading and growing your vocabulary. Study with the mindset that words you don't know can't be heard and can't be seen anyway.
