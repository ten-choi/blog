This blog didn't start here. It has moved a few times over the years, and each move taught me something about what I actually want from a place to write. Here's the short version.

2020: Tistory, in Korean

I started blogging in May 2020, during an internship where I was learning web development for the first time. I leaned heavily on other developers' blogs back then, and after getting that much help, I wanted to leave a record of my own. My first post explained what Node.js is and how to install it.

I wrote on Tistory, a popular Korean blogging platform, and kept at it for a few years. The blog eventually reached around 100,000 total views, which meant a lot to me.

Medium, in English

Over time it started to bother me that only Korean readers could ever find what I wrote. I wanted to share in English too, so I moved to Medium.

Early 2025: looking for something I could control

Around early 2025, Medium stopped issuing API tokens. If a platform is that reluctant about its API, there's no telling what it will change next, so I started looking around again. I tried dev.to for a few posts and compared it with Google Blogger, and came to one conclusion: being locked into a platform makes it hard to own your writing or customize anything.

2025: Markdown on GitHub, published to Blogger

So I changed the setup instead of the platform. Every post now lives as a Markdown file in a GitHub repository, and a small script publishes it to Google Blogger through its API. The writing is mine, versioned, and portable; Blogger is just where it's displayed.

For a while I ran more than one Blogger blog, including a separate one for language-learning notes.

2026: settling on Ten Dev Notes

In 2026 I brought everything together here. Posts from Tistory and my other blogs were imported, and in November 2026 the older Korean posts were translated into English. Those are tagged Archive: early notes, sometimes rough, but kept as a record of how I got here. If you only want the newer writing, use Browse → Hide archive in the menu.

Why I still write

AI has taken over a lot of the documentation I used to do by hand. These days I write less to share how-tos and more to organize my own thinking, because being able to explain a well-formed opinion to someone else is a skill worth building on its own.

Thanks for reading, whichever version of this blog you found first.
